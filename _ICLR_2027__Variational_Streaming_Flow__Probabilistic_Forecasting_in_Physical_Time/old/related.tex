\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Stochastic world modeling.}
Stochastic world models are necessary when an observed context does not
uniquely determine its continuation.  A deterministic predictor induces a
point-mass conditional law and therefore cannot represent a non-Dirac
$p_\star(\cdot\mid x_{a:k})$.  Moreover, under squared-error regression its
population optimum is the conditional mean,
$g^\star(x_{a:k})=\E[x_{k+1}\mid x_{a:k}]$, which may interpolate between
incompatible modes.  This ambiguity is central to visual world modeling, where
a single context may admit several semantically distinct futures
\citep{porcher2026flow}.  Related distributional prediction problems arise in
stochastic physical systems, where unresolved forcing induces irreducible
conditional variability \citep{pfister2026stocbench}, and in perturbation
modeling, where unobserved biological or experimental context changes the
distribution of valid responses \citep{yuan2026perturbdiff}.  Together, these
settings motivate learning the full conditional future law, with deterministic
dynamics recovered as the Dirac special case.
